## Lifespan: a life simulator, one year at a time

Thanks for testing! Each press of **Age Up** is a year of your life: things happen, you choose, and the choices come back later. Live it out, die, then carry on as one of your children.

### Install (about a minute)

Open **Terminal** (press ⌘ Space, type *Terminal*, press Return), paste this line and press Return:

```
curl -fsSL https://github.com/__REPO__/releases/latest/download/install.sh | bash
```

It downloads the app into the Applications folder in your home folder and opens it. To open it again later, press ⌘ Space, type *Lifespan* and press Return. Nothing else on your Mac is changed. If you run it again later, you get the newest version and your saved lives are kept.

### Remove it completely

When you're done, paste this into Terminal:

```
curl -fsSL https://github.com/__REPO__/releases/latest/download/uninstall.sh | bash
```

It removes the app, your saved lives, settings, caches and logs, and lists everything it removed.

### Getting started

- **New life** lets you build a person (country, city, birth year, family, traits, looks), or press **⚡ Random life**.
- The four tabs on the right are where you act: **Occupation** (school, jobs, research, fame), **Assets** (money, homes, cars), **Relationships** and **Activities**.
- Each year has a few dots of free time and work time: one dot per thing you do. Spend them, then press **Age Up** (or Space).
- The **❓** button at the top explains how each part of the game works.
- There's a cheat panel too: **Cheats › Open Cheat Panel** in the menu bar. Use it freely: it's a sandbox.

### Worth trying

- A life in **Slovenia**: pick your town (Slovenj Gradec, Ljubljana, Piran, Idrija… 55 of them), each with its own region, landmarks, food and local events. Yugoslav childhoods, independence in 1991, the tolar and the euro, Triglav and Planica. Assets › your town shows where you live, and you can move.
- **A life in science**: get a PhD and a research job, then run your own lab: projects, papers, grants, PhD students, conferences, tenure, patents and, maybe, a Nobel. The challenge “From Lab Coat to Laurels” starts you on that road.
